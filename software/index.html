Tools I use

These are codes written by other people that I have used in my research, each credited to its authors.

MSc thesis, 2021 to 2022

PARAM

By L. da Silva et al. (2006); T. S. Rodrigues et al. (2014, 2017)

A Bayesian code that compares a star's observed properties with a grid of stellar models and returns distributions of its mass, radius and age. I used it on synthetic stars and on 35 seismic Ariel targets.

How I used itWeb interface, INAF Padova

MSc thesis, 2021 to 2022

MESA

By B. Paxton et al. (2011) and the MESA developers

An open-source code that evolves a star through time by solving the equations of stellar structure. I worked with its evolutionary tracks as the model grid behind PARAM and in my thesis figures.

How I used itDocumentationSource code

MSc thesis, 2021 to 2022

BCCode

By L. Casagrande and D. A. VandenBerg (2014, 2018)

Tables and codes that convert the light a star emits in one filter into its total output, the bolometric correction. I used them for luminosities in my MSc thesis, with Monte Carlo sensitivity tests.

How I used itSource code and tables

MSc thesis, 2021 to 2022

YBC

By Y. Chen et al. (2019)

A database of bolometric corrections for many photometric systems, made for the PARSEC isochrones. I used it alongside BCCode to turn Gaia magnitudes into luminosities in my MSc thesis.

How I used itYBC database, INAF Padova

MSc thesis, 2021 to 2022

mwdust

By J. Bovy et al. (2016); dust map by G. M. Green et al. (2019)

A Python package that returns interstellar extinction for a direction on the sky and a distance, from published dust maps. I used it for the extinction of Ariel targets, favouring the 3D map of Green et al. (2019).

How I used itSource code

PhD, since 2025

TRILEGAL

By L. Girardi et al. (2005)

A model of the Galaxy that predicts the stars seen in a given field. I used it to generate synthetic populations of globular clusters for noise forecasts of the proposed HAYDN mission.

  1. May 2026 to present live

    Tellurion: a laboratory of interactive physics simulations

    Covers mechanics, electromagnetism, optics, quantum mechanics, relativity, statistical mechanics, fluid dynamics, condensed matter and astrophysics. Built during the PhD as a teaching resource.

    • Plain JavaScript modules without a framework, drawn in Canvas2D, SVG and WebGL2, on shared numerical engines with symplectic integrators and Markov chain Monte Carlo samplers.
    • Each simulation has invariant tests against thresholds in its specification, plus browser end-to-end and pixel-regression tests.
    • Optional course routes follow FCUP programmes. Public beta.

  2. Apr 2026 to present released

    Starfold

    Python package that finds groups in a table of measurements without labels, using UMAP, HDBSCAN and Optuna. It extends Neitzel et al. 2025.

    • Searches UMAP and HDBSCAN settings from the library defaults and scores each one without labels against a structureless copy of the table.
    • Refits the chosen setting on 32 random subsets to measure how often each group recurs, and returns 17 named flags for claims the evidence does not support.
    • GPU path through RAPIDS cuML with a CPU fallback, strict typing and 294 tests.
    • Matched or beat the library defaults on 8 of 10 benchmark tables, but on only 2 of 4 held-out tables.

  4. Apr 2026 to present in development

    ArqueoGal integration repository

    Prepares Gaia DR3 and APOGEE DR19 data, with provenance, for the models of the FCT ArqueoGal project. The stellar-label model (JANUS) and the population classifier (Starfold) are separate repositories.

    • Applies the Lindegren et al. 2021 parallax zero point, the Riello et al. 2021 G-band correction and the Mészáros et al. 2025 APOGEE DR19 label corrections.
    • Assembles extinction, distances and galpy kinematics, with a provenance file next to each product.
