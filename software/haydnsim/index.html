HAYDNsim

A simulation pipeline that builds synthetic stellar fields of crowded clusters and forecasts the photometric noise of each star, written to test the proposed HAYDN mission.

HAYDNsim is a Python package that builds synthetic stellar fields for the proposed ESA HAYDN mission and turns them into per-star inputs for the HAYDN Noise Estimator. It serves the mission proposal, which is described on its own page; this page is about the pipeline. The paper is in preparation, so this page keeps to what the poster and the talk below have already shown.

What it does

The pipeline takes a cluster from archive data to a noise forecast for every star in its field, as the schematic above shows stage by stage. In a cluster core the stars overlap, so a large share of the light in any one photometric mask belongs to the neighbours rather than to the target, and that share is what decides whether the mission can measure oscillations there.

I am the sole developer of HAYDNsim. I wrote it during a secondment in A. Miglio's group at INAF-OAS Bologna, between September 2025 and March 2026, and the noise runs were made on Deucalion and on the University of Bologna clusters. The repository is private.
