## The question

Stars like the Sun ring with sound waves driven by churning gas below the surface, and the tones reveal a star's mass, radius and age. In K dwarfs, smaller and cooler than the Sun, the oscillations move the surface by less than 10 cm/s, and space telescopes have detected them in only a few of these stars (Sect. 1, Fig. 1). Their strength tests the physics of convection and is needed to forecast what the *PLATO* mission will detect (Sect. 1).

## What the team did

The team measured how fast the surfaces of two K dwarfs about 6 parsecs (20 light years) away move towards and away from Earth. ESPRESSO, on ESO's Very Large Telescope in Chile, took a 25-second exposure about once a minute: of GJ 570 A over five nights in May 2024, and of GJ 783 A over six nights in September 2024 (Sect. 2). The team compared the results with 19 other stars, the Sun included, and with 3D simulations of their outer layers (Sect. 5).

## What came out

Both stars oscillate, with periods of three to four minutes. The team measured 6 oscillation modes (individual tones) in GJ 570 A and 24 in GJ 783 A, with peak amplitudes of 2.57 ± 0.52 and 4.90 ± 0.81 cm/s, against 16.0 cm/s for the Sun (Sects. 4.1 and 5.1).

Small, cool stars oscillate more weakly than the rule for Sun-like stars predicts. Amplitudes scale as (L/M)<sup>0.7</sup> above roughly 5300 K and as (L/M)<sup>1.5</sup> below, where L/M is luminosity over mass (Fig. 6). The simulations predict (L/M)<sup>0.9</sup> for K dwarfs and miss the drop (Sect. 5.2).

## My part

I carried out the GJ 570 A observations in visitor mode, meaning on site at the telescope. I was at ESO's Paranal Observatory from 4 May 2024 and observed from 6 to 12 May on behalf of the principal investigator, Tiago Campante, under his programme 0113.D-0255(B). Wind and overcast skies kept the telescope closed on the first night and for half of the fourth, leaving five usable nights and 2369 spectra (Sect. 2).
