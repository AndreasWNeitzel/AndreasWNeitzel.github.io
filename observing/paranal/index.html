observing Paranal, Chile · 4 to 12 May 2024

Observing at the Very Large Telescope

In May 2024 I spent nine days at ESO's Paranal Observatory in the Atacama desert and ran a campaign with the ESPRESSO spectrograph on the nearby star GJ 570 A. It was Tiago Campante's programme; I was the astronomer on site.

6 to 12 May 2024six visitor-mode nights, ESO run 113.26FX.002
ESPRESSOon one 8.2 m Unit Telescope, high-resolution mode
GJ 570 Aa K4 dwarf about 6 parsecs away
2,369 spectra25 s exposures, one about every 61 s, over five usable nights
2.57 cm/speak oscillation amplitude found, against 16.0 cm/s for the Sun

What I did

Visitor mode means the observer travels to the telescope and runs the observations with ESO's staff, instead of sending instructions for others to execute. For six nights I observed GJ 570 A with ESPRESSO, taking a short exposure about once a minute to follow the star's surface as it moved towards and away from us.

The weather took its share. High winds and cloud kept the telescope closed on the first night and for half of the fourth, which left five usable nights and 2,369 spectra. I did this on behalf of the principal investigator, Tiago L. Campante, under his programme 0113.D-0255(B); the proposal was his, not mine.

What the data showed

GJ 570 A rings like a bell with periods of three to four minutes, but very quietly: its surface moves by about 2.6 cm per second at the peak, a sixth of the Sun's value. Together with GJ 783 A, observed later in 2024, it shows that small, cool stars oscillate more weakly than the rule for Sun-like stars predicts, and 3D simulations of their outer layers do not reproduce the drop.

The results are in Campante et al. 2026, published in Astronomy and Astrophysics (doi:10.1051/0004-6361/202660350), where I am sixth of 24 authors. Read the plain-language explainer.
